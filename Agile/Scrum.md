## 🧠 **What is Scrum? 

**Scrum** is an **Agile framework** used to build software in **small, manageable parts**, delivering working features in short cycles (called **sprints**). It promotes:

- **Team collaboration**
- **Customer feedback**
- **Quick adaptation to changes**

---

## 👥 **Scrum Roles

### 1. **Product Owner (PO)**

- Represents the customer or business.
- Writes and prioritizes features/requirements in a list called the **Product Backlog**.
- Example: The PO says, _“We need to add a ‘Report User’ feature to improve safety.”_

### 2. **Scrum Master**

- A **coach/facilitator** for the team.
- Ensures the team follows Scrum values.
- Removes obstacles (ex: server down, unclear requirement).
- Example: If the test environment is not working, the Scrum Master escalates it and gets it fixed.

### 3. **Development Team**

- A **cross-functional team** of developers, testers, designers, etc.
- Builds and tests the product.
- Example: You, as a **manual tester**, verify the 'Report User' feature on Android, iOS, and Web platforms.

---

## 🧩 **Scrum Artifacts (Detailed)**

### 1. **Product Backlog**

- A master list of all desired features, enhancements, bug fixes, etc.    
- Managed by the Product Owner.
- Example items:
    - Add "Report User" button
    - Improve notification delivery time
    - Fix crash on login screen

### 2. **Sprint Backlog**

- A smaller list of items selected **from the Product Backlog** to be completed in the current **sprint** (typically 1–4 weeks).
- Created during **Sprint Planning**.

### 3. **Increment**

- The **working product** delivered at the end of a sprint.
- Should be **tested, functional, and potentially shippable**.
- Example: After testing the “Report User” feature, it is ready for release.
    

---

## 🔁 **Scrum Events (Detailed with Example)**

### 1. **Sprint**

- A fixed time period (usually **2 weeks**) where a small set of features is developed and tested.
- Goal: Deliver a **usable product increment**.

> 📌 Example: Your team plans a 2-week sprint to build and test the new “Report User” functionality on mobile apps.

---

### 2. **Sprint Planning**

- Held on the first day of the sprint.
- Team discusses **what to build and how to do it**.
- QA testers estimate test effort and dependencies.

> 🎯 Example: You estimate that it’ll take 2 days to write and execute test cases for “Report User” on both Android and iOS.

---

### 3. **Daily Scrum (Stand-up Meeting)**

- 15-minute daily sync-up.
- Each team member answers:
    1. What did I do yesterday?
    2. What will I do today?
    3. Any blockers?
        

> 💬 Example:  
> “Yesterday, I finished test cases for ‘Report User’.  
> Today, I will start test execution.  
> Blocker: I need access to a rooted Android phone for edge case testing.”

---

### 4. **Sprint Review**

- Held at the end of the sprint.
- Team **demonstrates** completed work to the Product Owner and stakeholders.

> 🧪 Example: You show that the “Report User” feature works correctly on Android, iOS, and Web after thorough testing.

---

### 5. **Sprint Retrospective**

- Internal team meeting.
- Discuss:
    - What went well?
    - What didn’t?
    - What can we improve?

> 🔁 Example:  
> “Testing went smooth, but we got dev builds late. Let’s ask devs to share early APKs next time.”

---

## 🎯 Real-Life Example: Scrum in a Manual Testing Context

Let’s say your team is assigned to implement a **"Dark Mode" feature** for a messaging app.

1. **PO adds** “Add Dark Mode” to the Product Backlog.
    
2. During **Sprint Planning**, you choose this task for the 2-week sprint.
    
3. You write test cases like:
    
    - UI visibility in dark mode
        
    - Switching between light/dark mode
        
    - Compatibility across Android versions
        
4. Every day in **Daily Scrum**, you update progress.
    
5. You **test the feature thoroughly** on multiple devices.
    
6. In **Sprint Review**, you demo that it works across platforms.
    
7. In **Retrospective**, the team agrees to improve coordination with UI designers earlier in the sprint.

## 📝 Summary

|Scrum Element|Purpose|Example in Testing|
|---|---|---|
|Product Owner|Defines & prioritizes work|Requests “Dark Mode” feature|
|Scrum Master|Removes blockers|Helps fix test environment|
|Dev Team|Builds & tests product|You test “Dark Mode” on all platforms|
|Product Backlog|Master list of work|All upcoming features, fixes|
|Sprint Backlog|Selected work for sprint|Dark Mode feature and related test tasks|
|Increment|Deliverable at sprint end|Fully tested “Dark Mode” ready for release|
|Sprint Planning|Plan the sprint|Decide what to test and how|
|Daily Scrum|Track progress|Share daily updates and blockers|
|Sprint Review|Demo completed work|Show test results for Dark Mode|
|Sprint Retrospective|Improve process|Discuss what went well and what to improve|
