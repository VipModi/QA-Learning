## 🔄 What is **Agile**?

**Agile** is a **project management approach** used in **software development** (and now many other industries), which focuses on:
- **Flexibility**
- **Iterative progress**
- **Team collaboration**
- **Customer satisfaction**

Instead of delivering the entire product at once (like Waterfall), Agile delivers work in **small, usable chunks** — allowing feedback and adjustments **throughout the process**.

---
## Agile Manifesto

The **Agile Manifesto** is a set of guiding values and principles for software development that emphasizes flexibility, collaboration, and customer focus. It was created in **2001** by a group of 17 software developers who wanted a better, more adaptive way to build software than traditional "waterfall" methods.

---

### ✅ **The 4 Core Values of the Agile Manifesto:**

1. **Individuals and interactions** over processes and tools
    
2. **Working software** over comprehensive documentation
    
3. **Customer collaboration** over contract negotiation
    
4. **Responding to change** over following a plan
    

> ⚠️ The right side still has value, but Agile prioritizes the left side more.

---
## 🧠 Key Principles of Agile (from the Agile Manifesto)

1. **Customer satisfaction** through early & continuous delivery.
    
2. **Welcome changing requirements**, even late in development.
    
3. Deliver working software **frequently** (every 1–4 weeks).
    
4. **Daily collaboration** between business and tech teams.
    
5. Build around **motivated individuals**.
    
6. Prefer **face-to-face communication** (or live communication).
    
7. Working software is the **primary measure of progress**.
    
8. Maintain a **sustainable pace** of work.
    
9. Continuous attention to **technical excellence** and good design.
    
10. Simplicity — the art of maximizing the work not done.
    
11. Teams **self-organize** to deliver better results.
    
12. Regularly reflect and **improve** (e.g., retrospectives).

## 📦 How Agile Works – In Simple Terms

|🏗️ Traditional (Waterfall)|🚀 Agile|
|---|---|
|Full product built first, then tested|Small features are built and tested continuously|
|Fixed plan, scope, timeline|Flexible scope and evolving requirements|
|Testing happens after coding|Testing happens throughout|
|Delivers only once at the end|Delivers frequently (every 1-4 weeks)|

## 📌 Core Agile Terms You Should Know

|Term|Meaning|Example|
|---|---|---|
|**User Story**|End-user feature description|“As a user, I want to mute a group chat.”|
|**Sprint**|Short time-boxed period (1–4 weeks) to deliver work|“Sprint 5: Apr 1–15”|
|**Product Backlog**|List of all desired features & bugs|Managed by Product Owner|
|**Sprint Backlog**|Items selected for the current sprint|From Product Backlog|
|**Daily Standup**|Daily 15-min team sync-up|What you did / plan to do / blockers|
|**Retrospective**|Team reflection after each sprint|“What went well? What can improve?”|
|**Burndown Chart**|Visual chart of remaining work|Should trend downward in a sprint|

## 🧪 Agile for Testers – Your Role as QA

As a tester in Agile:

- You’re involved **from the start** (requirement review, story grooming).
- You write test cases while dev is coding (parallel work).
- You test in **short sprints** – continuous, fast cycles.
- You help define **Acceptance Criteria** for stories.
- You join **daily standups, sprint reviews, retrospectives**.

---

## 🎯 Real-World Agile Workflow (Simple View):

1. **Product Owner** creates stories in backlog.
2. Team picks stories for the next sprint (planning).
3. Devs & testers work simultaneously.
4. Testers execute tests as stories are developed.
5. Bugs are raised & fixed within the sprint.
6. Sprint ends → Demo → Retrospective → Repeat.
